GRP07 — Build a Gem for Professional Work

4 points Group of 3–4 ~45 minutes
Session 8 · Chapter 6: Language and Conversation  |  Featured tool: Gemini Gems  |  Due: end of class
💡 WHAT THIS LAB IS Tonight your group builds a Gemini Gem for a real professional task — the kind of recurring work a financial analyst, an accountant, a web developer, an HR coordinator, or a project manager actually does week after week. The center of gravity is the instructions you write. As Chapter 6 puts it: the instructions are the heart of it. Tonight your group writes them deliberately, using a six-part scaffold — Role, Persona, Inputs, Outputs, Must Always, Must Never — and then tests whether the Gem actually works.

Group members & roles

Every member picks one role. All roles contribute — no coding is required for any of them.

RoleMember name
Lead Driver — runs Gemini on the shared screen, types/pastes the instructions, runs the tests
Data Interpreter — reads each Gem response out loud and judges whether it fits the professional use case
Presenter — delivers the share-out to the class
QA Reviewer — writes the misbehavior-attempt prompt and fills this worksheet

1Pick a professional role and a recurring task

✅ DO THIS — 5 min As a group, pick one professional role and one specific recurring task within that role. The Gem you build tonight should be one a real person in that job would actually use, on a real Tuesday, to save themselves 15 minutes. Pick from the menu below — or, if a member of your group actually does a different professional job, build for that instead.

1. Financial Analyst — Earnings Memo Drafter

Turns raw quarterly numbers and bullet observations into a polished earnings-summary memo in the firm's house voice.

2. Accountant (small business) — Transaction-to-Journal-Entry Assistant

Takes a plain-language transaction description and returns the proper account categorization, journal entry, and any flags.

3. Web Developer — Vague-Request-to-Dev-Ticket Translator

Converts a vague client request ("make the navbar pop") into a structured developer ticket with acceptance criteria and effort estimate.

4. Marketing Manager — Multi-Platform Social Rewriter

Takes one product feature description and drafts three social posts — LinkedIn (formal), Instagram (casual), X (concise) — in the brand's voice.

5. HR Coordinator — Job-Posting Harmonizer

Turns a bullet-pointed job description into a polished, compliant, inclusive job posting with consistent voice.

6. Real Estate Agent — Listing-Description Drafter

Takes raw property data (sqft, beds, baths, location, comps) and drafts a listing description in the agent's voice.

7. Project Manager — Meeting-Notes-to-Action-Items Extractor

Takes raw meeting notes and extracts: decisions made, action items with owners, blockers, and open questions for next meeting.

8. Teacher / Professor — Bloom's-Level Quiz Question Generator

Takes a learning objective and generates five scenario-based quiz questions at a specified Bloom's level.

9. Custom — A real job one of your members actually does

If a member of your group works in healthcare, retail, education, law, hospitality, the trades, or something else not listed above — and they have a recurring task that fits this lab — go that direction. The lesson is the same either way.

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? A Gem is only useful if it serves a real, repeated job. "Build a chatbot" produces nothing of value; "build the thing my colleague would actually use on Monday morning" produces the conversation that teaches you what good instructions look like. Naming the role and the task forces every decision that follows to answer a real question.

2Write the six-part instructions together

✅ DO THIS — 15 min Fill in each of the six sections below as a group. Be specific. "Senior accountant at a small Miami CPA firm" beats "accountant." "Direct, candid, no hedging" beats "professional tone." The whole demo you just watched was about one thing: you get clearer, not the AI smarter. This is where that work happens.

1. Role

Who is the Gem? Job title, level, and context. Give it a real position with real authority — not a generic label.

Example (Financial Analyst): "You are a senior financial analyst at a mid-cap consumer goods company. You have ten years of experience drafting quarterly earnings memos for the CFO."

2. Persona

Voice, tone, expertise level, formality. What does it sound like — confident or cautious, warm or terse, bilingual or English-only?

Example (Financial Analyst): "Precise, sober, plain-English. You never use jargon when a regular word will do. You don't hedge — when the numbers point one way, you say so."

3. Inputs it expects

What does the user give it — a transaction description, raw numbers, a vague client request, meeting notes? Be specific so the Gem knows what's coming.

Example (Financial Analyst): "The user will paste a small table of quarterly numbers (revenue, costs, margin, year-over-year change) plus two or three bullet observations about the quarter."

4. Outputs it produces

Format, length, structure. A memo? A bulleted list? Three social posts in three styles? Specify so every response looks the same.

Example (Financial Analyst): "Produce a one-page memo with four labeled sections: HEADLINE (one sentence), KEY DRIVERS (3 bullets), RISKS (2 bullets), OUTLOOK (one sentence). Total length: 200–250 words."

5. Must always do

The non-negotiables. Tone rules, format rules, ethics rules. Two or three lines.

Example (Financial Analyst): "Always cite the exact number from the source data. Always flag any assumption you made. Always include year-over-year change in percentage terms."

6. Must never do

The guardrails. Things that would make this Gem a liability if it did them.

Example (Financial Analyst): "Never invent numbers the user didn't provide. Never make forward-looking projections beyond the next quarter. Never replace the CFO's judgment on what counts as material."

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Writing every section as one short line — "be professional," "be accurate," "be helpful." Those are the weak instructions from the demo, just spread across six boxes. Sentences with the words "always," "never," and concrete numbers, names, or formats beat adjectives every time.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Before Step 3, every one of the six boxes has at least two sentences in it, and your Persona and Must Never boxes describe behaviors specific to this professional role — not generic AI behavior. If a box reads like it would fit any Gem, rewrite it.

3Build the Gem and test it

✅ DO THIS — 15 min
  • The Lead Driver opens gemini.google.com, finds Gems in the sidebar, and clicks Create new Gem.
  • Name the Gem something descriptive — "Earnings Memo Drafter" or whatever fits your role.
  • In the Instructions field, paste your six sections from Step 2 (keep the labels).
  • Save the Gem and open a chat with it.
  • Run four test prompts as a group and fill in the table below. The QA Reviewer writes the misbehavior-attempt prompt — the prompt designed to make the Gem step out of its role or ignore a "must never" rule.
Test type Prompt we sent Gem's response (summarize in 1–2 sentences) Did it work?
1. Standard request — a typical input a real user would send.
2. Standard request — a second typical input, slightly different.
3. Edge case — an unusual but valid input the Gem should still handle.
4. Misbehavior attempt — try to make it ignore a "must never" or step out of its role.
💡 WHY THE FOUR TESTS Two standard tests check that the Gem does its job. The edge case checks whether the instructions cover what real users will actually throw at it (they always send something unexpected). The misbehavior attempt checks whether the guardrails hold — if they don't, the Gem is a liability the moment a real person uses it.

4Refine and document

✅ DO THIS — 5 min Based on your four tests, make at least one change to the instructions. It might be tightening a "must never," adding a missing output format, sharpening the persona — whatever the test results pointed at. Then write down what you changed and why.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Trying to fix a misbehaving Gem by prompting it harder in the chat. That's the wrong reflex — and the chapter and the demo both warn against it. The instructions are where the behavior lives. Edit the instructions, not the prompt.

5Verdict and share-out prep

✅ DO THIS — 5 min As a group, answer the verdict honestly. Your Presenter delivers a 60-second report.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Your Presenter will read your final instructions aloud to the class. Confirm Step 2's six boxes are filled in, Step 3's four test rows are filled in, and Steps 4 and 5 are complete. If any box is blank, fill it in now.

What to submit

💡 ONE WORKSHEET PER GROUP This completed worksheet is your submission. It must include your role and task (Step 1), all six instruction sections (Step 2), all four test rows (Step 3), your refinement (Step 4), and your verdict (Step 5).
✅ HOW TO SUBMIT In your browser, choose File → Print → Save as PDF. Confirm every section is visible — nothing cut off — then upload the PDF to the GRP07 assignment in Canvas. One submission per group, with every member's name in the roles table. Due by the end of class.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Gemini Gems doesn't appear in the sidebar.Try refreshing once. If it's still not there, click "Explore Gems" first — sometimes the Create option appears from inside that view. Worst case, the Lead Driver shares their screen and one member of the group runs the Gem from their own account.
The Gem keeps adding "As an AI language model..." preambles.Add a line to "Must Never": Never refer to yourself as an AI or a language model. You are a [role]. Save and retest.
The Gem ignored a "must never" in our misbehavior test.That's a real finding — record it in the test table as a failure. Then in Step 4, sharpen that line: be specific about what not to do and what to do instead. "Never give legal advice" is weaker than "If asked for legal advice, respond exactly: 'I can't give legal advice — please consult an attorney.'"
Our outputs are way longer than we asked for.Move the length requirement from "Must Always" to the "Outputs" section, and be specific: "Total length: 150–200 words. Do not exceed this." Save and retest.
The Gem is too generic — sounds like default Gemini.The Persona section is doing the most work here. If it reads like a job description, rewrite it as character: "you don't hedge," "you push for outcomes," "you don't open with pleasantries." Behaviors beat traits.
One member doesn't have a Google account.The Lead Driver runs the Gem on their account, with the whole group watching the shared screen. Every member can still contribute fully to writing the instructions and judging the responses.
