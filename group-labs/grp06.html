💡 WHAT THIS LAB IS
Tonight you watched YOLO draw bounding boxes around objects — tight rectangles snapped to about 80 fixed categories. Now your group tries a completely different way to understand an image: you'll upload photos to Google AI Studio and ask a multimodal AI (Gemini) to describe what it sees, in plain language. Same task — understanding images — two fundamentally different approaches. You'll compare where each one wins, then try to break Gemini on purpose. Honest evaluation is the point, not a perfect score.
✅ WHAT YOU NEED
A laptop or phone with a browser. Go to aistudio.google.com and sign in with a Google account. Pick any available Gemini model — version doesn't matter for this lab. Each group member should have 3–5 images ready on their phone or laptop (everyday objects, food, places, signs).

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE
Writing "Gemini is better" or "YOLO is better." Neither is true — they're built for different jobs. The point isn't to crown a winner; it's to notice which approach fits which task. Keep both answers specific.

3Now try to break Gemini

✅ DO THIS — 12 min
Every system has weak spots. Divide these three tests among your members and run all three.

Task 3A — Tricky image. Upload something challenging — blurry, odd angle, dim light, or uncommon — and ask Gemini to describe it.
Task 3B — Impossible question. Upload any image and ask something it can't know from the picture alone: "What does this food taste like?" · "How old is this person?" · "What happens next?" · "Who took this photo?"
Task 3C — Text-in-image. Upload an image with visible text (a sign, menu, label, book cover) and ask: What text can you see in this image?

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? — the honesty question
Remember the confidence numbers from tonight's demo — every YOLO box came with a percentage like "person 88%," the model's built-in signal for "how sure am I." Gemini usually gives you no number at all — and on the impossible question, it will often state a confident guess in the same calm voice it uses for facts. Watch for that. Which approach is more honest about what it doesn't know?
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE
For the tricky-image test, picking a photo that's hopelessly bad — a tiny, pitch-black smudge. A model failing on something no one can read teaches nothing. "Tricky" means a person reads it fine but one thing is pushed: odd angle, low light, partial cover.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK
Before Step 4, your three test rows hold actual Gemini responses — what it really said, not "I think it would say…". If a row is blank or guessed, go run it for real.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
AI Studio won't load, or a sign-in fails.Try a different member's Google account on the shared screen. If it's still blocked, your instructor will post a fallback link in the Zoom chat — switch to it and keep going.
Gemini refuses an image or returns a safety message.Swap in a different, neutral image (no faces of strangers, no sensitive content). Note that it refused — that's a finding about guardrails, not a failure of the lab.
Gemini answers with a giant wall of text.Narrow the prompt: "List only the objects, one per line" or "Answer in one sentence." Controlling the output is part of using the tool well.
Gemini states a guess as if it were fact (Step 3B).That's the lesson, not a bug. Record it word-for-word — it's strong material for your Task 4B diagnosis.
The group can't agree whether an answer is "right."Go back to your scenario in Step 4. Right is a context question — a loose description might be plenty for a blind-user app and nowhere near enough for a security alert.
Uploading from a phone is slow.Have one member AirDrop or email the photos to the Lead Driver, who uploads from a laptop — faster than one-at-a-time from phones.
