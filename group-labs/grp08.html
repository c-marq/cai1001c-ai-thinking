GRP08 — Generate Music in MusicGen — and Listen for the Seams

4 points Group of 3–4 ~45 minutes
Session 9 · Chapter 7: Generative Media  |  Featured tool: Hugging Face Spaces (MusicGen)  |  Due: end of class
💡 WHAT THIS LAB IS Tonight your group generates short background music for a real use case in MusicGen, iterates through three prompts to make it better, and then does the harder work: listens for the seams — repetition, awkward transitions, instruments that sound like banjos but aren't quite — and asks the chapter's harder question: if we shared this on social media as background music, how would a listener even know it was AI-made? That question, and the provenance answer to it, is the real point of the lab. The Space is free, runs in the browser, and needs no account.

Group members & roles

Every member picks one role. All roles contribute — no coding is required for any of them.

RoleMember name
Lead Driver — runs MusicGen on the shared screen, types prompts, plays results
Data Interpreter — listens analytically, names what's working and what isn't on each version
Presenter — delivers the share-out to the class (will play your best clip)
QA Reviewer — fills the worksheet and leads the provenance discussion in Step 4

1Pick a use case and open MusicGen

✅ DO THIS — 5 min As a group, pick one real use case for short background music — the kind a person would actually use, on a real Tuesday, for a real project. Pick from the menu below, or use one a group member actually needs.

1. Podcast intro music (15 sec)

The first thing a listener hears before the host's voice — sets the show's tone instantly.

2. Workout video background (loop-friendly)

High-energy, steady tempo, no lyrics — for a 30-second exercise reel.

3. Hold music for a small business phone line

Pleasant, neutral, not annoying after 60 seconds. Harder than it sounds.

4. Lullaby for a baby

Gentle, slow, soothing — something a parent would actually press play on at 11 pm.

5. Yoga or meditation session background

Ambient, no sharp transitions, no melody pulling attention.

6. 30-second product ad background

Energetic, builds to a small "moment," fits a brand vibe of your group's choice.

7. School spirit or event hype video

Upbeat, anthemic, makes a crowd cheer.

8. Study or focus session background

Steady, no lyrics, doesn't pull attention from reading.

9. Custom — a real music need a group member actually has

A small business someone in the group runs, a video they're editing, a podcast they host — go that direction.

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? AI-generated music is judged the same way the vision model was last week — fitness for purpose. "Pretty good music" is not the question; "good enough for hold music callers won't hate after 60 seconds" is. Naming the use case forces every prompt decision and every judgment call that follows to answer a real question.

2Generate three versions with refinement

✅ DO THIS — 15 min Generate three versions, each refined based on what the previous one sounded like. Keep duration at 15 seconds for all three. After each generation, the Data Interpreter says one sentence on what's working and one sentence on what isn't — fill those in immediately so the next prompt can react.

Version 1 — first attempt

Specify genre, tempo, instruments, mood. Be more specific than "happy music."

Version 2 — refined

Change one or two things in the prompt based on what V1 sounded like. Don't rewrite from scratch — refine.

Version 3 — final

One more refinement — this is the one you'll share with the class.

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Treating each version like a totally fresh start. The skill is refinement — keeping what worked, changing what didn't, paying attention to which words in the prompt actually moved the result. If V2 and V3 sound completely different from V1 in every dimension, you weren't refining — you were rolling dice.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Before Step 3, you have three real generated clips (not just three prompts), all three "what's working / what isn't" rows are filled in, and the group agrees on which version is V3 — the one you'll share. If any of that isn't true, finish it now.

3Study the seams in your best version

✅ DO THIS — 10 min Take your V3 (the version you'd share). Play it three more times as a group — not background-listening, focused listening. Each time, listen for a different thing. Fill in the table with at least three seams — flaws, oddities, or "tells" that hint it was machine-made. Rate honestly how hard each was to spot.
What we noticed (the seam) Type — pick one: repetition / weak ending / fake-sounding instrument / structural / pitch or rhythm / other How hard to spot — Obvious / Took a listen / Subtle
💡 WHY THE "HOW HARD" COLUMN MATTERS The chapter's myth-vs-reality says: "I would recognize a fake — it would sound off" is not a safe defense, because the tells are unevenly distributed and shrinking. The seams you find now, in 2026, on a free Space, are the ones that will not be there next year. The "Subtle" ratings are the honest ones — those are the future of all of this.

4The provenance check — the hard question

✅ DO THIS — 5 min Imagine your group posted V3 on TikTok, Instagram, or a podcast feed as background music with no caption saying it was AI. The QA Reviewer leads this discussion. As a group, answer the two questions below in two-to-three sentences each.

5Verdict and share-out prep

✅ DO THIS — 5 min As a group, deliver the verdict honestly. Your Presenter plays V3 for the class and gives a 60-second report.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Confirm: three versions recorded, at least three seams in the Step 3 table with honest difficulty ratings, both provenance answers written, verdict written. Your Presenter knows which clip is V3 and how to play it for the class.

What to submit

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
The MusicGen Space won't load.Refresh once. If it's still down, your instructor will post a fallback Space link in chat — switch and continue.
Generation is taking forever (60–90 seconds).That's the free-tier queue. While it runs, the group can be writing the "what's working / what isn't" row from the previous version — the worksheet is designed so there's always something to fill in.
The audio doesn't play, or plays without sound.Check that Zoom's "Share computer sound" is enabled. On a phone, check that the device is not on silent mode.
The music has a weird abrupt ending or loops on itself.That is exactly what Step 3 is about — record it as a seam, not a problem to fix. The seams are the lesson.
The Space won't let us download the audio file.For the share-out, the Presenter can simply share their screen and play it from MusicGen directly. You don't need to download anything.
The group can't agree on whether V3 "works."Go back to your Step 1 use case. Works is a context question — V3 might be perfect for a workout reel and terrible for a yoga session. The use case decides.
We finished early — what now?Generate a fourth version pushing in a totally different direction (different genre, different mood) to feel the model's range. Or write a one-sentence prompt your group thinks would break the model, and try it.
