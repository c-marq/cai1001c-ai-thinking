GRP09 — Group App Build · The Rehearsal for the Final

4 points Group of 3–4 ~90 minutes
Session 10 · Chapter 8: AI in the Real World  |  Featured tool: Google AI Studio — Build Mode  |  Due: end of class
💡 WHAT THIS LAB IS Tonight your group builds a small but real working app in AI Studio Build Mode — the same tool your instructor just demoed. Pick a small real problem, describe an app precisely, watch AI Studio build it, test it honestly, refine the description, and ship a working prototype. This is not just a lab — it is your dress rehearsal for the individual Final App Builder Project due Wednesday at 11:59 PM. By the end of class your group should have a clickable preview on screen and you should know exactly how the loop feels.

Group members & roles

Every member picks one role. All roles contribute — no coding is required for any of them.

RoleMember name
Lead Driver — runs AI Studio Build Mode on the shared screen, pastes the description, runs refinements
App Designer — leads the writing of the app description, owns the user-flow decisions
Tester — clicks every control in the preview, finds what breaks, reports honestly
QA Reviewer — fills this worksheet and prepares the share-out

1Pick a tiny problem worth building for

✅ DO THIS — 5 min As a group, pick one small, real, useful task from a member's life. The best apps tonight are not ambitious — they are specific. Pick from the menu, or use something a member actually needs.

1. Tip and bill splitter

Enter bill, pick a tip percentage (15/18/20), split N ways. Show tip and total in large text.

2. Unit converter for a trade

Inches ↔ centimeters, gallons ↔ liters, °F ↔ °C, with a preset list of common conversions.

3. Study-session planner

Enter a reading list and exam date; the app splits it across the days available.

4. Event countdown / deadline tracker

Add events with names and dates; show days/hours remaining; sort by soonest.

5. Quick-quote estimator for a small business

Pick a service, enter a few inputs (e.g., sqft, hours), get a price estimate. Useful for tradespeople, tutors, photographers.

6. Recipe scaler

Paste a recipe; enter desired servings; the app rewrites ingredient amounts proportionally.

7. Household chore-rotation tracker

Enter family members and chores; the app rotates assignments week-to-week with a clear view of who does what.

8. Medication-schedule organizer

Add medications with doses and times; the app lays them out across a day and flags overlaps. (No real medical data tonight — use test data.)

9. Custom — a tiny tool someone in the group actually needs

If a member has a recurring task that fits this scope, build that instead. The lesson is the same either way.

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Picking something ambitious — "a social media platform for students," "an AI tutor for calculus." Tonight is 90 minutes. The chapter is clear: the best capstones are not ambitious; they're specific. A working tip splitter beats a vaporware tutor every time.

2Write the app description together

✅ DO THIS — 15 min Fill in the five-part scaffold below as a group, then assemble it into a single description to paste into AI Studio. Be specific. The demo you just watched succeeded because the description was paragraph-long, not three-word. Same skill as the Gemini Gems instructions you wrote two weeks ago — applied to a different tool.

1. What the app is and who it's for

One sentence. "A tip-and-bill splitter for groups eating out who want to split fairly without arguing."

2. What the user enters

List the inputs. Be specific about format. "Bill amount in dollars, tip percentage (chosen from buttons 15/18/20), number of people splitting."

3. What the app shows / does

The output. "Tip amount in dollars, total with tip in large text, per-person amount split equally."

4. Look and feel

Color scheme, button style, mobile-friendly? "Clean, modern, with a green accent. Mobile-responsive. Large readable numbers."

5. The edge cases — what should happen when something is off

"If the bill is empty, show a hint. If the number of people is zero, do not divide. Round per-person amounts to the nearest cent."

3Build it in AI Studio Build Mode

✅ DO THIS — 15 min
  • The Lead Driver opens aistudio.google.com and signs in with their Google account.
  • In the left sidebar, click Build — not Prompts, not Playground.
  • Click New / Build a new app.
  • Paste your assembled description from Step 2 into the prompt field.
  • Click Build and wait — generation usually takes 30–90 seconds.
  • Watch the preview appear. Don't touch it yet. Step 4 is the test phase.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Clicking on the preview the second it appears. The Tester role is to wait until everyone is ready, then click through systematically. Random fast clicking hides what's broken.

4Test honestly — click everything, try strange inputs

✅ DO THIS — 10 min The Tester runs the preview with the group watching. Try the app as a stranger would. Click every button. Try empty inputs, very large numbers, very small numbers, zero, negative numbers. Document at least four findings — what worked, what didn't.
What we tested (input or action) What the app did Worked? (Y / partial / N)
💡 WHY HONEST TESTING MATTERS Section 8.4 of the chapter warned plainly: a working preview is not a finished app. Build Mode produces polished-looking output in seconds, which is exactly what makes it easy to call the first version "done." It almost never is. The strange inputs you'd never try as the developer are the ones a real user will hit on day one. Test like you mean it.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Before Step 5: at least four real test rows are filled in with honest results. If every row says "worked perfectly" you haven't tested the hard inputs. Try the empty input, the absurd input, the wrong-type input. Then refine.

5Refine — at least one improvement

✅ DO THIS — 10 min Based on your testing, write one refinement instruction for AI Studio and apply it. Send it into Build Mode and let the app regenerate. Things that make good refinements: handling an empty input, adding a reset button, making a number bigger, improving an error message, fixing math on an edge case.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Asking AI Studio to "fix the bugs" or "make it better." Build Mode is not psychic — it works on specific, named instructions. "If the bill input is empty, show a friendly message instead of NaN" is a refinement. "Make it better" is a wish.

6Verdict and share-out prep

✅ DO THIS — 5 min As a group, answer honestly. Your Presenter delivers a 60-second report to the class — name the problem, show the app, name the best refinement.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Confirm: problem picked (Step 1), five-part description and assembled version written (Step 2), screenshot of working preview (Step 3), four real test rows (Step 4), refinement instruction and result (Step 5), verdict written (Step 6). If any of those are blank, fill them in now.

What to submit

💡 ONE WORKSHEET PER GROUP This completed worksheet is your submission. It must include your problem, your assembled description, your screenshot, your four test rows, your refinement, and your verdict.
✅ HOW TO SUBMIT In your browser, choose File → Print → Save as PDF. Confirm every section is visible — including the screenshot — then upload the PDF to the GRP09 assignment in Canvas. One submission per group, with every member's name in the roles table. Due by the end of class.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Build Mode doesn't appear in the AI Studio sidebar.Make sure you're signed into your Google account at aistudio.google.com (not just Gemini). If still missing, click "Apps" or "Studio Apps" — Google occasionally renames the entry point.
The Build button isn't doing anything when clicked.Refresh the page. If it stalls again, try a different browser tab in incognito mode. Google's cache occasionally tangles with extensions.
Generation has been running for more than two minutes.It's stuck. Refresh and re-paste your description. Don't sit silently waiting — your time matters more than this one attempt.
The preview generated but looks completely wrong.Your description was too vague. Don't argue with the preview in chat — go back to Step 2, sharpen the description, and rebuild. The skill is editing the description, not editing the result.
The app works but it's ugly / off-brand.Send a refinement just for the visual: "Make the primary color teal instead of blue. Make the button text larger." Build Mode handles UI changes well.
One member doesn't have a Google account.The Lead Driver runs Build Mode on their account, with the whole group watching the shared screen. Every member still contributes to writing the description and judging the result.
We finished early.Send a second refinement. Test it. Or run a totally different app description for 10 minutes as practice for the individual Final.
