GRP03 — Build a Grounded Notebook

4 points Group of 3–4 ~40 minutes
Session 3 · Chapter 2: Digital Literacy and Data  |  Featured tool: NotebookLM  |  Due: end of class
💡 WHAT THIS LAB IS In the demo you watched NotebookLM answer only from chosen sources and cite them. Now your group does it. The goal is not a perfect notebook — it is to curate sources you can defend, ground an AI in them, and see for yourselves what the tool does when it is pushed past what it knows. Honest work over polished work: this is low-stakes by design.

Group members & roles

Every member picks one role. All roles contribute — coding is not required for any of them.

RoleMember name
Lead Driver — runs NotebookLM on the shared screen
Data Interpreter — leads the five-question source test
Presenter — delivers the share-out to the class
QA Reviewer — checks citations and fills this worksheet

1Choose your research question

✅ DO THIS — 5 min As a group, pick one real research question from a member's field, studies, or life. It must have a genuine answer — not an opinion. Good: "What causes coral bleaching in the Florida Keys?" Not a question: "Is climate change bad?"

2Curate four to six sources

💡 THE FIVE-QUESTION SOURCE TEST (Section 2.2) Run every candidate source through these before you accept it. Keep only the ones that pass.
  1. Who made it, and what do they know?
  2. How current is it?
  3. What evidence backs it — can you trace it?
  4. What purpose does it serve, and who benefits?
  5. Does it hold up in a second, independent source?
✅ DO THIS — 15 min Find sources together. For each one, tick the questions it passes, then write one sentence on why your group trusts it. Fill at least four source cards; six if you have them.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Accepting a source because it is first on the results page or simply "looks official." Ranking is not trust. A source that fails question 3 (no traceable evidence) or question 5 (you cannot confirm it anywhere else) does not go in — write it on the rejected list instead.

🛑 STOP AND CHECK Before moving on, confirm: you have 4–6 accepted sources, every one has at least most of the five questions ticked, every one has a trust sentence, and you have written down what you rejected. If a source has only one or two ticks, talk about whether it really belongs.

3Build the notebook

✅ DO THIS — 10 min
  • Go to notebooklm.google.com, sign in, and click Create new.
  • In the Sources panel, add all of your accepted sources.
  • In the Chat panel, ask at least three questions about your topic.
  • Click the numbered citations — confirm each one lands on a real passage.

4Test its honesty

✅ DO THIS — 5 min Ask NotebookLM one question your sources do not cover — something off-topic for your source set. Watch carefully what it does.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Asking a question your sources actually do cover and getting a normal answer. Pick something genuinely outside the material — that is the whole point of this step.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Did the notebook tell you the answer was not in your sources, or did it answer anyway? Either way, describe it honestly above — there is no wrong observation here, only an unrecorded one.

5Prepare your share-out

✅ DO THIS — 5 min Your Presenter will give the class a 60-second report. Fill this in together so they are ready.

What to submit

💡 ONE WORKSHEET PER GROUP This completed worksheet is your submission. It must contain: your research question, your accepted sources with a trust sentence for each, and your three sentences on what NotebookLM did when asked something outside its sources.
✅ HOW TO SUBMIT In your browser, choose File → Print → Save as PDF. Check that nothing is cut off, then upload the PDF to the GRP03 assignment in Canvas. One submission per group — put all group members' names in the roles table above. Due by the end of class.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Can't sign in to NotebookLM.Use a personal Google account or your MDC Google login. The tool is free and open to anyone 13 or older — no separate sign-up needed.
A website source won't add.Some pages block import. Open the page, copy its text into a Google Doc, and add the Doc instead — or pick a different source that passed the five-question test.
Answers have no citation numbers.Make sure your question is about the sources, and that the sources actually finished loading in the Sources panel before you asked.
The notebook answered a question we thought was outside our sources.Your sources probably touch the topic indirectly. Pick a clearly unrelated question and try again.
Pasted screenshot won't show when printing.Click inside the dashed box first, then paste. If it still clips, paste it again as a smaller image.
