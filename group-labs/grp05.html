GRP05 — Train a Classifier in Teachable Machine

4 points Group of 3–4 ~40 minutes
Session 5 · Chapter 4: Machine Learning  |  Featured tool: Teachable Machine  |  Due: end of class
💡 WHAT THIS LAB IS Tonight your group trains a real machine learning model — an image classifier — in Teachable Machine, with no code. You will teach it from your own webcam examples, then deliberately break it and diagnose why it failed. The goal is to feel the chapter's central lesson first-hand: a model is only as good as its training data. Teachable Machine is free, runs in the browser, needs no account, and has no age limit.

Group members & roles

Every member picks one role. All roles contribute — no coding is required for any of them.

RoleMember name
Lead Driver — runs Teachable Machine and the webcam on the shared screen
Data Interpreter — leads the training-data plan, owns the variety decisions
Presenter — delivers the share-out to the class
QA Reviewer — runs the break-it tests and fills in this worksheet

1Choose a classification task

✅ DO THIS — 5 min As a group, pick a simple task a webcam image classifier could decide — two or three classes. Name the classes now. Some options:
  • Physical objects — mug vs. phone vs. notebook; pen vs. marker.
  • Hand signs — rock vs. paper vs. scissors; thumbs-up vs. thumbs-down.
  • Expressions — smiling vs. neutral.
Keep it appropriate, simple, and something every member can present to a webcam in class.
💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? Classification means sorting a new case into one of a few predefined categories. Choosing the task and naming the classes before collecting any examples is the same first move a data scientist makes — you decide what the model is for, then you gather data for it.

2Plan your training data

✅ DO THIS — 5 min Before recording anything, decide as a group and write it down: how many examples per class, and how you will make them varied — different angles, distances, lighting, backgrounds, and group members. A plan made on purpose beats examples grabbed carelessly.
💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? This is the chapter's biggest practical lesson: the model's quality lives in its training data. The algorithm is fixed — the examples you give it are the part you actually control.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Recording 30 near-identical frames from one angle in one light. That is not 30 examples of the concept — it is basically one example copied 30 times. A model trained that way learns "this object, here, in this light," not the object itself. Variety is the whole point.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Before you record: your group can name the task, name the classes, and has a written plan for varied training data. If the variety plan is still only spoken, write it down now.

3Train it

✅ DO THIS — 15 min
  • Go to teachablemachine.withgoogle.com and choose New Project → Image Project → Standard image model — no account needed.
  • Create one class per category and rename each one meaningfully.
  • For each class, hold the object or sign to the webcam and record examples — follow your Step 2 plan for both the count and the variety.
  • Click Train Model. In seconds it learns the boundary between your classes.
  • Test it live in the preview panel — show the webcam each class and watch the confidence scores.

4Break it on purpose

✅ DO THIS — 10 min Now make your model fail — honestly. Try a new lighting condition, a new background, a different angle, a different person, or an object it was never trained on. Find a situation where it predicts the wrong class or wobbles with low confidence. Then diagnose why, using this chapter's vocabulary — training data, variety, overfitting, generalization.
💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? A model that only works in the exact conditions it was trained in has not learned the concept — it memorized your examples. Breaking it on purpose is how you find out whether it generalizes to anything new.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE "It works perfectly — we can't break it." You probably have not pushed hard enough. Change two things at once (new background and new lighting), or show it something outside all of its classes. Every model has a breaking point; finding it is the assignment.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK You have a working model, your final examples-per-class count is recorded, and you found one real failure with a why written in chapter vocabulary. If the "why" just says "it didn't work," sharpen it — name what about the training data caused the failure.

5Prepare your share-out

✅ DO THIS — 5 min Your Presenter gives the class a 60-second report: demo the model live, show your one failure, and explain its cause. Fill this in together.

What to submit

💡 ONE WORKSHEET PER GROUP This completed worksheet is your submission. It must include your trained-model screenshot, your final examples-per-class count, the one-paragraph model description, and three sentences on one failure and its training-data cause.
✅ HOW TO SUBMIT In your browser, choose File → Print → Save as PDF. Confirm your screenshot is not cut off, then upload the PDF to the GRP05 assignment in Canvas. One submission per group, with every member's name in the roles table. Due by the end of class.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Teachable Machine can't see our webcam.Allow camera access when the browser asks. Make sure no other app or tab (Zoom, another meeting) is already holding the camera, then reload the page.
The model predicts the same class no matter what we show it.Usually one class has far more examples than the others, or the classes look too similar to the camera. Balance the example counts and pick more visually distinct classes.
Confidence scores jump around wildly.The background or lighting is doing the work, not the object. Re-record with cleaner, more varied examples — adding a class for "nothing"/empty background can also help.
It works on us but fails on a classmate.That is the lesson, not a bug — the model generalizes only to what is in its training data. Add that classmate's examples and retrain.
A pasted screenshot won't show when printing.Click inside the dashed box first, then paste. If it still clips, paste a smaller image.
