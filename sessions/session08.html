Session 8 — Language and Conversation

Session 8 of 11 Week 5 Chapter 6
Monday, June 8, 2026  ·  6:00–9:20 PM
Talk to a modern AI assistant and it can feel like understanding. It picks up your tone, follows your point, remembers what you said three messages ago. Chapter 6 is about what is actually happening underneath — a model predicting one word at a time — and what that fact means for trust, fluency, and the line between a tool you'd hand a real task to and one you wouldn't. Then in the second half, you build something of your own: a custom Gemini Gem for a real professional task, designed with instructions you write deliberately.

Before class

What we're doing

Block A — Lecture & guided demo

Quick recap of Chapter 5, then Chapter 6's core ideas: tokenization, embeddings as "meaning by context," the transformer's attention mechanism, and the honest truth at the center — an LLM predicts plausible words, which is why fluency is not understanding and not truth. Then a live build: starting from a deliberately weak set of Gem instructions, watching the assistant be vague and unhelpful, and rewriting the instructions with a real persona and constraints until the Gem becomes a tool you would actually use.

Block B — Breakout lab

After the break you move into groups. Each group picks a professional role (financial analyst, accountant, web developer, marketing manager, HR coordinator, real estate agent, project manager, teacher — or one a group member actually does), names a recurring task in that role, and builds a Gem for it. The center of gravity is the instructions — the six-part scaffold you fill in together is the lab.
