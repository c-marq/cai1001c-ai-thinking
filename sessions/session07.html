Session 7 — Computer Vision

A computer never sees a photograph the way you do. It receives a grid of numbers — and every capability in Chapter 5 is math performed on that grid. Tonight we walk both halves of the chapter: the four jobs machines do with images, the contested case of faces, and the bridge to machines that do not just recognize images but generate them. In the second half, your group puts a real vision model to work and tries to break it on purpose.

Before class

What we're doing

Block A — Lecture & guided demo

Quick recap of Chapter 4's classifiers, then Chapter 5's core idea: an image is a grid of numbers, and every vision task is math on that grid. Four jobs (classify, detect, segment, track), the contested case of facial recognition, and the cross from recognition to generation. The live demo runs an object-detection Hugging Face Space on real photos — clear and broken on purpose — and ends with a short look at Nano Banana generating an image from a prompt, as the bridge to Chapter 7.

Block B — Breakout lab

After the break you move into groups. Each group picks a real-world use case for an object-detection model — accessibility, security, retail, traffic, healthcare, or environment — feeds ten of its own images through the Space (five clear, five hard), diagnoses the failures using Chapter 5's vocabulary, and decides whether the model is fit for that purpose.
