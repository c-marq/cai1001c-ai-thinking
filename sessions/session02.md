## Block A — Lecture + Live Walkthrough

**Topics covered:** What digital literacy is and why it is a survival skill in the age of AI; the [five questions for judging any source](../ch02-digital-literacy-data#2-2-judging-a-source); [where data comes from](../ch02-digital-literacy-data#2-3-where-data-comes-from) and the Internet of Things; and [grounding an AI in sources you choose](../ch02-digital-literacy-data#2-5-meet-notebooklm-an-ai-that-cites-its-sources).

The class follows this rhythm:

**Lecture** — the instructor teaches digital literacy and the five-question habit for judging a source, then explains what makes NotebookLM different from Gemini: it answers *only* from sources you give it, and cites every claim.

**Live demo — NotebookLM, end to end.** The instructor demonstrates the full workflow from [Section 2.6 of the chapter](../ch02-digital-literacy-data#2-6-hands-on-walkthrough-research-that-cites-its-sources):

- Creating a notebook and **adding curated sources** — the step that decides the quality of everything after it
- **Asking questions in the Chat panel** and clicking the numbered citations to land on the exact passage
- **The Studio panel** — turning sources into a written briefing, flashcards, a quiz, a **mind map**, and an **Audio Overview** (podcast-style discussion), plus generated **video** and **slides**
- **How to prompt the Studio apps** for useful, on-topic outputs
- **The outputs-to-notes-to-source loop** — saving a generated output back into the notebook as a new note
- **What NotebookLM will not do** — asking it something the sources do not cover, and watching it decline rather than invent

The walkthrough the instructor demonstrates is [Chapter 2, Section 2.6: Hands-On Walkthrough](../ch02-digital-literacy-data#2-6-hands-on-walkthrough-research-that-cites-its-sources). Follow along in the chapter if you want the steps in front of you.
:::

## Block B — Discussion + Breakout Lab

### Use cases and ideas

Before the lab, a short whole-class discussion: where would *grounded* research actually help you? The chapter lists a few starting points — a healthcare student loading assigned readings, a small-business owner comparing vendor contracts, a tradesperson pulling an exact procedure from a manual, anyone facing a dense lease or policy. Bring your own. The goal is to leave with a real use case in mind before you open the tool.

### This session's lab

The lab is the **[Breakout Lab: Build a Grounded Notebook](../ch02-digital-literacy-data#breakout-lab-build-a-grounded-notebook)** from Chapter 2. Working in a group of three or four, you will pick a real research question, curate four to six sources — running each through the [five-question test](../ch02-digital-literacy-data#2-2-judging-a-source) before you accept it — build a NotebookLM notebook, and test what it does when asked something outside its sources.

:::{tip} 📋 Lab instructions
The full lab — steps, timing, and what to submit — is in [Chapter 2: Breakout Lab](../ch02-digital-literacy-data#breakout-lab-build-a-grounded-notebook). Submit your group's deliverable on the Canvas assignment page for **GRP02**.
:::
