The presentation format — 5 minutes per student

TimeWhat you cover
0:00–0:30The problem. Who is this app for, and what real problem does it solve? One sentence per question.
0:30–2:30The live demo. Show the app actually working. Walk through the user flow you designed. Click the buttons. Use the strange inputs you found in testing. Show one thing that works beautifully and one thing that's still imperfect.
2:30–3:30The build story. Your most useful refinement and what it taught you. The one moment where your judgment, not the AI's speed, was the thing that mattered.
3:30–4:30The ethics moment. One specific ethics question your app raises (bias, privacy, fairness, accountability, transparency, oversight) and how you addressed it — or chose not to.
4:30–5:00Close. Who would use this in real life, and what would you build next?

Before class

What we're doing

Block 1 & 2 — Live presentations

10–15 students × 5 strict minutes each = roughly 80–90 minutes of presentations split across two blocks with a break. Listen actively to your classmates — every demo you watch is another concrete answer to "what can a real person, with no programming experience, build with AI in 2026?" The presentation order will be posted in the Zoom chat at the start of class. If you have a documented absence and submitted a recorded video, your video will be played in your presentation slot.

Block 3 — Course wrap (35 min)

The closing. We review the six ethics questions you'll carry into the rest of your life (Section 8.3's "ethics check"), each student shares one habit from the course they're bringing with them, and the night ends with a real thank-you. The tools in this course will date; the way of thinking will not. Mind the machine — to the end, and past it.
