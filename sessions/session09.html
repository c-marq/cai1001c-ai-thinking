Session 9 — Generative Media

Session 9 of 11 Week 5 Chapter 7
Wednesday, June 10, 2026  ·  6:00–9:20 PM
In Chapter 5 a machine turned a sentence into an image. In Chapter 6 it turned a prompt into paragraphs. Tonight we follow that thread to where it now leads: a machine can turn a sentence into a voice, a piece of music, or a short video — moving, sounding clips of things that never happened. This is genuinely new in human history, and this chapter is not about alarm — it is about understanding, which is the only real protection. In the second half, your group generates music from a prompt and listens hard for the seams.

Before class

What we're doing

Block A — Lecture & guided demo

Recap of Chapter 6's "fluency is not understanding," then Chapter 7's unifying idea: text, image, audio, and video generation are not four different technologies — they are one engine pointed at four kinds of training data. We look hard at how AI generates audio (synthetic speech, music, voice cloning) and video (temporal consistency, the deepfake problem), and at provenance — the only durable defense. Then a live MusicGen demo on a deliberately ordinary prompt, a refinement, and a brief Veo glimpse to mark where the frontier is.

Block B — Breakout lab

After the break you move into groups. Each group picks a real use case for short background music, generates three versions in MusicGen with refinement between each, listens analytically for the seams (repetition, awkward endings, unrealistic instruments), and decides whether the result is fit for the use case — and how a listener would even know it was AI-made.
