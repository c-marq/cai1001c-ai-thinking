The course is worth a total of **400 points.** Your grade is the total points you earn divided by 400. Grades are based solely on points earned and are **not rounded up** — for example, 319 points is a C, not a B.

## Point distribution

| Category | What it is | Points | Share |
|---|---|--:|--:|
| In-Class Group Projects | 10 projects × 4 pts | 40 | 10% |
| Non-Guided Take-Home Projects | 4 projects × 30 pts | 120 | 30% |
| Reading Quizzes | 4 quizzes × 25 pts | 100 | 25% |
| Final App Builder Project (capstone) | one capstone | 120 | 30% |
| Attendance | 2 pts per session, capped at 20 | 20 | 5% |
| **Total** | | **400** | **100%** |

Chapter readings from *Mind the Machine* are assigned before each class but are **not graded** — they prepare you for the in-class demonstrations and labs.

## The assessments at a glance

- **In-Class Group Projects** — the breakout lab at the end of each chapter, done in randomly assigned groups during the class lab block. Low-stakes: credit is earned by being present, contributing to the deliverable, and taking part in the share-out.
- **Non-Guided Take-Home Projects** — four take-home assignments that extend the in-class group project with a deeper, independent dive. Each one adds an ethics-or-bias dimension to the reflection.
- **Reading Quizzes** — four short, scenario-based quizzes (ten questions, a 20-minute limit), each covering two chapters of the textbook.
- **Final App Builder Project** — the capstone; its rubric is below.
- **Attendance** — two points per class session.

## Capstone rubric — Final App Builder Project (120 points)

For your capstone you build a small, working AI-powered app that solves a real problem, using **Gemini Canvas**, and then analyze how you did it. The rubric is short on purpose. Each line describes what *full marks* looks like; partial work earns partial credit. The guiding rule: **a rough app with sharp thinking beats a polished app with shallow reflection.** (The full project brief is in the separate capstone handout.)

### Part 1 · The App — 60 points

- **It works, and it's real — 40 pts.** The app runs (live, in the Canvas preview, or in your recorded demo) and tackles a genuine problem, not a toy. It connects to an AI capability or idea from the course, and you can name which chapter.
- **You made it yours — 20 pts.** You directed the AI tool through several rounds of refinement and made deliberate choices about scope, layout, and which features to include.

### Part 2 · The Demo — 20 points

- **A clear 5-minute demo — 20 pts.** A screen recording, or a slide deck with screenshots, that shows your app, demonstrates one feature, and explains four things: the problem it solves, what works and what doesn't, one challenge you hit, and one course idea that shaped it. Honest about what broke.

### Part 3 · The Reflection — 40 points

*One page. Specific beats long.*

- **Your project — 12 pts.** What you built, who it's for, the AI capability and chapter it draws on, and one thing you'd change with more time.
- **Ethics in design — 18 pts.** One *specific* ethical choice you made while building — something you added, limited, or disclosed — named to one of the six ethics principles from §8.3 (bias, privacy, fairness, accountability, transparency, human oversight) or to a chapter. "AI has risks" earns nothing here; specificity is the point.
- **Directing AI — 10 pts.** What building with AI taught you: what it did well, where it fell short, and how it differed from training a classifier yourself in Chapter 4.

**Submit three things:** your app (a share link, recording, or slides), your demo, and your one-page reflection. **One firm rule:** never pay for anything — every tool you need is free.
