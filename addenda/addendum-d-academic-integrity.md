This is a course about artificial intelligence, and you will use AI tools in almost every session. That makes one question unavoidable: *when is using AI part of the work, and when is it a way of avoiding the work?* This addendum answers it.

## The principle

Academic integrity here rests on a single idea: **the thinking, the judgment, and the choices in your work must be your own.** AI is a tool you *direct* — not a substitute for understanding the material. A tool can draft, suggest, and speed things up. It cannot be curious for you, decide what matters, or take responsibility for what you submit. Those remain yours.

So the test is not "did you use AI?" In this course you are *expected* to. The test is: **did you do the thinking the assignment was designed to make you do?**

## Using AI is encouraged — here is where

Much of this course is learning to use AI well. The following is not only allowed, it is the assignment:

- Using the chapter's featured tool in labs and projects, as directed.
- Using an AI assistant to explain a concept, brainstorm ideas, draft a starting point, or help debug.
- Using AI to *build* your capstone app — directing it to generate and refine code is the whole point of that project.

## Where the line is

These cross it:

- **Submitting work that is not yours** — another student's app, or an AI's unedited output, presented as your own.
- **Letting AI do the thinking an assessment is meant to measure.** Reading Quizzes test *your* understanding of the chapters; complete them yourself, without AI assistance. A reflection is a record of *your* thinking; pasting in AI-written paragraphs as if they were your own defeats its purpose — and is easy to spot.
- **Contributing nothing to a group project** while taking the group's credit.

## Disclosing your use of AI

When AI meaningfully helped with a piece of work you submit, **say so.** A short, honest note is enough — for example: *"I used Gemini to debug the date calculation and to suggest clearer wording for the introduction."* You do not need to log every keystroke; you do need to be honest about substantive help.

Disclosure is expected on the **capstone** (your reflection already asks how you directed the AI) and on the **Non-Guided take-home projects**. It is not required on the in-class labs, where AI use is the visible, assigned activity. When you are unsure whether something needs disclosing, the answer is simple: **disclose it, or ask.**

To put it the way the book does: using AI is not cheating. *Hiding* it, when disclosure is expected, is. Honesty about your tools and your process is itself part of what this course is teaching — and it costs you nothing, because the work is graded on your thinking, not on whether you had help.

## If something goes wrong

If you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is appropriate, ask your instructor *before* you submit — not after. Honest questions are always welcome. Violations of this policy are handled under Miami Dade College's Student Code of Conduct and academic-integrity procedures.
