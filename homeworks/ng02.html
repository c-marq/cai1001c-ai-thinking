NG02 — Going Deeper: When Grounding Isn't Enough

30 points Individual Optional
Session 3 · Chapter 2: Digital Literacy and Data  |  Due: Sunday, 11:59 PM
📓 WHAT THIS IS In the group lab you built a notebook grounded in sources you trusted. This take-home pushes the other way: you will deliberately add one weak source and watch what happens — then reflect on what grounding does and does not protect you from. Work on your own. Use your group's notebook from GRP02 as the starting point, or rebuild a quick one if you would rather.
💡 HOW THIS IS GRADED Honest observation earns the points. "I expected X, but the notebook actually did Y, and that surprised me because Z" is exactly the kind of answer this assignment rewards. A guess dressed up as a confident answer is not. There is a full rubric at the end.

1Add one weak source

✅ DO THIS Find a source that fails the five-question test from Section 2.2 — for example, an anonymous post, an outdated page, a claim with no traceable evidence, or content from someone with a clear stake in selling you something. Add it to your notebook alongside the trustworthy sources.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Choosing a source that is merely boring rather than genuinely weak. You want one that clearly fails the test on purpose — that contrast is what makes the rest of the assignment work.

2See what it does to the answers

✅ DO THIS Ask your notebook a question the weak source would influence — something that source has an opinion or claim about. Read the answer and check the citations: notice whether the weak source gets cited, and how its claims sit next to the trustworthy ones.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Before the reflection, make sure you can answer this in your own head: did the weak source make the answer look any less trustworthy — or did it blend right in, cited just like the good sources? Hold onto that observation; it is the heart of Part 3.

3Reflection — about 300 words

💡 ADDRESS ALL FOUR QUESTIONS
  1. NotebookLM grounded its answer in your sources and cited them — but did grounding make the answer trustworthy?
  2. Whose judgment was actually responsible for the quality of the result?
  3. Could a curated source set still be biased even if every source in it is individually "credible" — for instance, if all of them share the same viewpoint?
  4. How would you guard against that?
