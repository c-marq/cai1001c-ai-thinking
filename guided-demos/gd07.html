GD07 — Recognize and Generate: A Vision Model in Motion

Session 7 · Wednesday, June 3, 2026  |  Chapter 5: Computer Vision  |  Demo tools: Hugging Face Spaces + Nano Banana (Google)
Where this runs: Block A, after the Chapter 5 lecture. Paced for roughly 20 minutes. This demo covers both halves of Chapter 5 — recognition with a real object-detection Space, and generation with Nano Banana — previewing what students do in the lab while building the bridge to Chapter 7 (Generative Media).

Before class — set up the demo

The demo lands only if students have just heard the "image is a grid of numbers" idea and are ready to see math do work on it. Stage two browser tabs and two photos before students arrive.

  • Open the assigned Space ahead of time and confirm it loads in your browser: huggingface.co/spaces/Xenova/yolov9-web. The first load fetches the model into your browser — once loaded, it runs offline for the rest of the demo.
  • Fallback Space if the primary is down: huggingface.co/spaces/atalaydenknalbant/Yolo11
  • Have two real images ready on your phone or in a folder:
    • Clear: a busy outdoor scene with several COCO categories — a street with cars, people, traffic lights, maybe a dog. The model should hit this confidently.
    • Hard: something the model will struggle with — an unusual angle, deep shadow, or an out-of-distribution subject (a Florida iguana on a wall, a piragua cart, a guava on a kitchen counter, an oddly-cropped close-up).
  • Sign in to aistudio.google.com with the Nano Banana image model selected and a fresh prompt window open.
  • Mirror your screen; keep Chapter 5's Figure 5.2 (image as numbers) open in a third tab for the framing moment.

The Demo

1Frame the problem — an image is numbers⏱ 0:00 · ~2 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Pull up Chapter 5's Figure 5.2. Walk the zoom: photo → grid of pixels → each pixel is one number for grayscale, or three for color. Point at the numbers.
🗣️ SAY "Hold on to one fact: when a computer 'sees' an image, it receives numbers. Nothing else. Tonight you watch two models do two opposite things with that idea. One finds patterns in the numbers — that's recognition. The other builds a new grid of numbers from your words — that's generation. Same chapter, two doors."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Resist the urge to talk about CNNs or transformers here. The chapter deliberately doesn't. The teaching idea is pixels are numbers, vision is math on numbers, and that one sentence carries the whole demo.
2Recognition — run a clear image⏱ 0:02 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Open huggingface.co/spaces/Xenova/yolov9-web. Upload your clear photo. Read the bounding boxes and the confidence scores out loud, one by one. Point to each box as you say its label and percentage.
🗣️ SAY "Look at what it did. It drew a rectangle around each thing and labeled it. And next to each label is a number — the model's confidence. Not certainty. Confidence. It is not 'seeing' the street. It is running math on a grid of numbers and reporting that the numbers in this region look 95% like the training examples labeled 'car.' Section 5.2 of the chapter, live."
⚠️ WATCH OUT The first load takes a few seconds while Transformers.js downloads the model into the browser. Narrate it instead of waiting in silence: "The model is downloading into my browser right now. That's why this runs without a server queue — once it loads, it lives here."
3Recognition — break it on purpose⏱ 0:06 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Upload your hard image. Let students see the failures: a missed object, a wrong label, a confident label on something absurd, or nothing detected at all. Read the confidence numbers anyway.
🗣️ SAY "Look at what it got wrong. A category it never saw in training — it picks the closest match it knows, often confidently, often wrong. Or the box drifts. The machine is not seeing wrong. The numbers in this image don't look like the numbers in its training examples. That is the limit of the model, named honestly. Remember Chapter 4's accuracy trap — a confident number is not the same as a correct one. That's tonight's lab in one slide."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Resist the urge to explain away a wrong answer. A confident wrong answer is the chapter's best teaching moment. Name it plainly: "It says skateboard. There is no skateboard. That is what overconfident classification looks like when the data shifts." An imperfect run beats a perfect one every time.
4Generation — Nano Banana⏱ 0:10 · ~6 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Switch to Google AI Studio with Nano Banana selected. Type a specific, sensory prompt: 
A photorealistic photograph of a Miami palm tree at golden hour,
shot on an iPhone, slightly overexposed, warm light.
Wait for the image. Display it next to a real Florida photo you have on hand.
🗣️ SAY "Same chapter, opposite direction. The recognition model just now was finding patterns in numbers. This model is putting patterns into numbers. Diffusion is the word from Section 5.4 — it starts from random noise, a grid of nonsense numbers, and refines that grid step by step toward the words I wrote. The output is a brand-new photograph of a thing that was never photographed. Compare the two side by side. Could you, honestly, tell which one came from a phone and which from a prompt? That is the world Chapter 7 lives in — and it's the world your students live in already."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Not every generation comes out photorealistic. If it looks plasticky or off, do not redo it — name it: "That faint plastic look is the model's blend of millions of training images, visible. Now imagine that same imperfect blend depicting a real person in a real place — that's Section 5.6's deepfake concern, in our hands."
5Land the point — and hand off to the lab⏱ 0:16 · ~3 min
🗣️ SAY "Two takeaways. First — a computer never sees the world. It does math on numbers. That is why a vision model is more consistent than a tired human inspector, and also why it can be broken by changes that wouldn't fool you for a second. Second, and bigger — the line between photographed and generated is now math we can do in two browser tabs, for free. The information world Chapter 2 described has changed for good, and your only real defense is understanding how these systems actually work. That defense is what tonight's lab builds."
👉 DO ON SCREEN — transition Close AI Studio. Leave the Hugging Face Space open and post the link in the Zoom chat. "Your turn — same Space I just used, your own images, your own use case." Pull up GRP06 and send students to breakout rooms.
⚠️ WATCH OUT — if asked "can we use Nano Banana in the lab?" Be clear: tonight is the recognition lab — putting a vision model to work and finding where it breaks. Generation gets its own night in Chapter 7. Keep them on the Space.
