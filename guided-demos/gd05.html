GD05 — Teachable Machine: Train a Real Classifier

Session 5 · Wednesday, May 27, 2026  |  Chapter 4: Machine Learning  |  Demo tools: Teachable Machine + TensorFlow Playground
Where this runs: Block A, after the Chapter 4 lecture. Paced for roughly 28 minutes. Both tools tonight — Teachable Machine and TensorFlow Playground — are free, browser-based, and have no age limit, a change from last session's 18+ demo tool. This is still an instructor-led walkthrough; students get their own hands-on time with Teachable Machine in the breakout lab.

Before class — set up the demo

This demo only lands if students watch a model fail and then watch the same model improve — with nothing changing but the data. Set it up so you can stage that on purpose.

  • Open teachablemachine.withgoogle.com in one tab — no account, no sign-in needed.
  • Decide your two demo classes ahead of time and have the objects on the desk. Simple and physical works best — for example a Mug and a Phone. Two classes is plenty.
  • Test the classroom webcam and screen mirroring before students arrive — the demo is live video.
  • Open playground.tensorflow.org in a second tab for the back half of the demo.
  • Keep the Chapter 4 page open in a third tab — you will point back to Sections 4.2 and 4.3.
  • Plan to be deliberately sloppy on the first training pass (Card 2): few examples, one angle, one light. The brittleness that causes is the whole lesson.

The Demo

1Frame it — we are opening the box⏱ 0:00 · ~3 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Pull up Teachable Machine, full screen. Hold up the mug and the phone. Don't touch the tool yet — just set the stage.
🗣️ SAY "The lecture said classification is just drawing a boundary between groups, and that the classifiers making real decisions about people are not black boxes. Time to prove it. In about two minutes, with no code, I am going to train a working machine learning model on these two objects — and then I am going to break it on purpose, so you see exactly where its quality comes from."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Resist re-teaching the five algorithms here — that was the lecture. This card is the doing part. Keep it short and get to the webcam.
2Create the classes and add examples — the lazy way⏱ 0:03 · ~6 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN
  • Choose a New Project → Image Project → Standard image model.
  • Rename the two classes meaningfully — Mug and Phone.
  • For each class, hold the object to the webcam and hold Record — but be deliberately lazy: grab only a handful of frames, from one angle, in the current lighting.
🗣️ SAY "These webcam frames are the training data. Watch how little care I am taking — same angle, same light, just a few shots. Everything from Chapter 1 said more and more varied examples make a stronger model. I am doing the opposite, on purpose. Hold that thought."
⚠️ WATCH OUT The Teachable Machine layout is stable, but if a control has moved, find the equivalent — the flow is always: create classes, add example images, train, preview. Also: keep captured frames appropriate, and don't record a student's face without asking.
3Train it, test it — and break it⏱ 0:09 · ~5 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Click Train Model — in seconds it learns the boundary between your two classes. Test it live in the preview panel: show the mug, show the phone, read the confidence scores aloud. Then break it — change the angle, move to a different background, dim or brighten the light. Watch the confidence wobble or flip to the wrong class.
🗣️ SAY "It worked beautifully on exactly what I showed it — and the moment I change the lighting, it falls apart. It did not learn 'mug.' It learned 'a mug at this angle in this light.' This is Section 4.3 live: a model that memorizes its examples instead of learning the pattern. The honest word for it is brittle."
⚠️ WATCH OUT A thin model sometimes still guesses right — if it refuses to fail, push harder: a hand partly over the object, a cluttered background, a new angle. An honest failure is the lesson; don't fake one, but do hunt for the real one.
4Retrain it honestly — change only the data⏱ 0:14 · ~5 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Add many more examples to each class — different angles, distances, lighting, backgrounds, even a second person holding the object. Click Train Model again. Now re-run the exact hard tests that broke it in Card 3.
🗣️ SAY "Same tool. Same algorithm. Same two classes. The only thing I changed is the training data — more examples, more variety — and look how much steadier it is. This is the single most important practical lesson in the whole chapter: a model's quality lives in its data. A biased or careless dataset becomes a biased or careless model, wearing a confident number."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Don't rush this card to save time — this contrast is the demo. If you must trim, trim Card 1, not this one.
5Switch tools — watch a network learn in TensorFlow Playground⏱ 0:19 · ~5 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Open playground.tensorflow.org and press play — a small neural network starts separating two colours of dots; watch the boundary form. Then switch the dataset to the spiral: with the default setup the network struggles. Add a hidden layer or two and more neurons, and watch the boundary gain the flexibility to wrap around the spiral.
🗣️ SAY "This is the neural network from Section 4.2, learning right in front of you. A simple, straight-ish boundary cannot catch a spiral — but stack more small units in layers and the boundary can bend into almost any shape. That flexibility is exactly what makes neural networks powerful, and exactly what makes them harder to read than a decision tree."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Don't dive into the feature toggles or the math — this is an AI-literacy class. The visible idea — a boundary forming and bending — carries the whole point.
6Push too far — see overfitting, then hand off⏱ 0:24 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN On the spiral, pile on neurons and let it run a long while. Point at the two loss numbers: the training loss keeps falling while the test loss starts rising, and the boundary stops being a clean curve and starts contorting around individual dots.
🗣️ SAY "That divergence — training score improving, test score getting worse — is overfitting, made visible. The network has stopped learning the pattern and started memorizing dots. And it ties straight back to our mug model: the brittle first version overfit its few same-angle photos. Whether it is a webcam classifier or a neural net, the failure is the same — memorizing instead of learning."
👉 DO ON SCREEN — transition Close both tabs. "Now it is your turn — same tool, your own classifier." Pull up the Group Lab (GRP05) and send students into breakout rooms to train, break, and diagnose a model of their own.
⚠️ WATCH OUT — if asked "what counts as enough data?" Be honest: there is no single number. It depends on how varied the real world is. The lab's whole point is for students to feel the difference between too little and enough — let them discover it rather than handing them a target.
