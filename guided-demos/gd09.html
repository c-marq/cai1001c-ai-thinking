GD09 — Generating Music — and Glimpsing the Frontier

Session 9 · Wednesday, June 10, 2026  |  Chapter 7: Generative Media  |  Demo tools: Hugging Face Spaces (MusicGen) + Veo (state-of-the-art glimpse)
Where this runs: Block A, after the Chapter 7 lecture. Paced for roughly 20 minutes. The MusicGen portion previews the same tool students will use in GRP08 — a deliberately different prompt so it doesn't spoil their lab. The Veo glimpse is demonstration only (18+ and paid) and frames where the frontier is, ending on the chapter's moral argument: the durable defense is provenance, not flaw-spotting.

Before class — set up the demo

Three things ready in three tabs. Audio in a classroom needs care — confirm sound works in the Zoom share before class begins.

  • Tab 1 — MusicGen Space. Open huggingface.co/spaces/facebook/MusicGen ahead of time and confirm it loads. Smoke-test Tuesday — Spaces go up and down. If it's down, post a fallback Space link in Zoom chat at class start.
  • Tab 2 — Veo glimpse. If you have access to Veo (Gemini Pro or Google AI Studio with Veo enabled), have a short prompt ready to generate live. If not, have one or two pre-saved Veo clips from a previous session, the Google DeepMind Veo showcase page, or a public demo. Either path works — what matters is the moment of "look at what video generation can do in 2026." Have the clip ready to play with audio.
  • Tab 3 — Chapter 7's Figure 7.2 (one engine, many media) and Figure 7.4 (SynthID + Content Credentials) open for the framing and landing moments.
  • Confirm Zoom audio share is enabled — "Share computer sound" must be checked, or your students will see your screen and hear nothing. This is the single most common failure mode for this demo.

The Demo

1Frame — one engine, many media⏱ 0:00 · ~2 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Pull up Figure 7.2 from Chapter 7 — the diagram of one engine fanning out to text, image, audio, and video. Point at the engine, then at each output.
🗣️ SAY "Hold one fact for the next twenty minutes. Every generative AI you have heard of works the same way: train on a massive collection of one kind of media, learn the patterns, generate from a prompt. Text in Chapter 6. Images in Chapter 5. Tonight, audio and video — same engine, new training data. There is no fourth secret. So when you watch what happens next, do not be impressed by the magic. Be alert to the seams — because the seams are where this technology still leaks."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Resist the urge to explain diffusion or transformers in depth here. The chapter already did that work in Chapter 5 (image diffusion) and Chapter 6 (LLMs). Tonight is about applying that understanding to two new media. One engine. Many media. That sentence is the frame.
2MusicGen — a deliberately ordinary prompt⏱ 0:02 · ~5 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Open huggingface.co/spaces/facebook/MusicGen. In the prompt field, type a deliberately weak prompt: 
happy upbeat music
Set duration to 15 seconds. Click Generate. While it runs (about 60 seconds in a free Space), narrate what's happening: "Behind that progress bar, a model is doing what every generative AI does — sampling patterns it learned from a vast library of music, conditioned by my three words, building a brand-new audio file fifteen seconds long. Same engine you saw with images. New training data." When it finishes, play the clip.
🗣️ SAY "Three words in. Listen to what came out. It is music. There is melody. There is rhythm. And it sounds… vaguely competent? Maybe a little bland. The prompt I wrote was three words — 'happy upbeat music' — and three words gave the model nothing to commit to. Generic in, generic out. This is the same lesson Chapter 1 taught us about prompts: the model cannot read your mind. What's about to change is not the model — it's me, getting clearer about what I want."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Generation may take 30–90 seconds on a free Space. Do not fill the silence with apologies. Use the wait to narrate what's happening, or to point at Figure 7.2 again. The wait is part of the demo — students will experience the same latency in their lab.
3Refine the prompt — then listen for the seams⏱ 0:07 · ~5 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Replace the prompt with a specific one. Type it slowly so students see the structure: 
Upbeat acoustic indie folk, 110 bpm, banjo and tambourine,
joyful summer road-trip feel, warm and bright.
Set duration to 15 seconds. Click Generate. When it finishes, play the clip — then play it a second time and ask the class to listen analytically.
🗣️ SAY "Same model. Same Space. Same fifteen seconds. Completely different result. That happened because I gave the model a genre, a tempo, instruments, a feel, and an adjective for the mood. The model's range was always there — I just told it which part of the range I wanted. Now listen for the seams. Did it loop on itself? Was the ending abrupt or natural? Do those instruments sound like real banjos and tambourines or like something like them? Is there structure — a beginning, a middle, an arrival — or does it just play? Those are the tells of generative audio in 2026. They are shrinking. Listen while you still can."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Do not pick a prompt that overlaps with what students will likely write in GRP08. Avoid "study music," "podcast intro," "yoga," "lullaby" — those are on the lab menu. Pick a use case the lab won't cover: road trip, sports highlight, holiday theme, retro arcade.
4Veo — where the frontier is⏱ 0:12 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Switch to Tab 2 — your Veo tab. Either generate a fresh clip live from a prepared prompt (something cinematic and specific, e.g., "a slow-motion close-up of rain falling on a bronze statue at dusk, golden hour, photorealistic, cinematic") or play a pre-saved clip you have ready. Play with audio on. Play it twice — once for impact, once for analysis.
🗣️ SAY "This is Veo, Google's high-end video model. It is not something you have access to in this class — Veo is 18+ and requires a paid plan, so this is demonstration only. But I want you to see where the frontier is in 2026. Watch what it did. It generated several seconds of moving footage from a sentence. The light is consistent across frames. The physics of the rain looks real. The texture of the bronze looks like bronze. This did not exist three years ago. Now watch a second time and look for the seams — the temporal consistency glitches from Section 7.3. A finger that subtly morphs. A background that shifts when it shouldn't. The seams are still there. They are closing fast. That is the world Chapter 7 wants you to think clearly about — not because video AI is bad, but because the line between recorded and generated is now a line you cannot see with your eyes alone."
⚠️ WATCH OUT — if you don't have Veo access Skip the live generation. Play a pre-saved clip from a public source (Google DeepMind's Veo showcase page has shareable examples). Name it plainly: "This was made by Google's Veo. I'm playing a clip Google published. It's exactly what one of these tools produces from a sentence today." The medium is the lesson, not the live click.
5Land on provenance — and hand off to the lab⏱ 0:16 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Switch to Tab 3 — Figure 7.4 (SynthID + Content Credentials). Point at the invisible watermark on the left, the origin label on the right.
🗣️ SAY "Here is the part of this chapter I will not let you miss. The MusicGen clip I made tonight and the Veo clip Google made — could you, honestly, tell either was AI-made if you came across it scrolling? Maybe yes today. Probably not next year. So how does an honest information world survive this? Not by everyone becoming a forensic expert. That race cannot be won. The answer is provenance — invisible watermarks like SynthID built into the media at the moment of creation, and Content Credentials, the tamper-evident origin label attached to the outside. Plus — and this is the link back to Chapter 2 — the old habit of checking the source. Your eyes are not the defense. The defense is the system. That is what this chapter, and tonight's lab, are really about."
👉 DO ON SCREEN — transition Close Veo. Leave MusicGen open and post the link in Zoom chat: huggingface.co/spaces/facebook/MusicGen. Pull up GRP08. "Your turn — pick a real music use case, generate three versions, listen for the seams, decide if it's fit for purpose." Send students to breakout rooms.
⚠️ WATCH OUT — if asked "can we use Veo in the lab?" Be direct: no. Veo is 18+ and paid; tonight's hands-on tool is MusicGen, free and no account. Veo was the glimpse, not the assignment.
