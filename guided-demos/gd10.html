GD10 — Build a Professional Headshot Generator

Session 10 · Monday, June 15, 2026  |  Chapter 8: AI in the Real World  |  Demo tool: Google AI Studio — Build Mode (with Nano Banana)
Where this runs: Block A4, after the Ch 8 compressed lecture, the AI for Oceans activity, and the Final App Builder Q&A. Paced for roughly 30 minutes. This is a demonstration only — students do not follow along here. They will do their own version in GRP09 right after the break. The demo's job is to show them that describing a polished app produces a polished app, in real time, with no code.

Before class — set up the demo

This demo lands because students see something genuinely impressive come into existence in 90 seconds. Confirm everything works in advance — Build Mode demos look spectacular when they work and confusing when they hiccup.

  • Sign into aistudio.google.com ahead of class. In the left sidebar, locate Build — confirm you're not in Prompts or Playground.
  • Have 2–3 test photos ready on your phone or in a folder — selfies, casual portraits, anything with a clearly visible face. You'll upload them in the live preview after the app builds.
  • Have the full app description prompt ready to paste — the one from your published guide, with the detailed photographic specifications for Nano Banana. Loading it from a text file or a tab is faster than typing it live.
  • If you've run this demo before, consider opening last term's deployed version in a separate tab as a fallback in case Build Mode is slow tonight. Naming what the finished app does, then building it, is the demo's spine — having an existing version as a safety net means you can keep narrating even if generation hangs.
  • Mirror your screen; close any Gmail or personal tabs you don't want students to see when you switch contexts.

The Demo

1Frame — describing an app is "vibe coding"⏱ 0:00 · ~3 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Open aistudio.google.com. Show the left sidebar. Point at Build — not Prompts, not Playground. Click Build. Show the interface. Click New / Build a new app.
🗣️ SAY "This is Build Mode. It is not the chat window. It is not the playground where we test prompts. This is something else — a place where you describe an application in plain English and the AI builds a real, working, deployable web app. People call this vibe coding. You bring the idea, the judgment, and the testing; the AI handles the code. You will not write a line of HTML, CSS, or JavaScript tonight. By the end of class, every one of your groups will have one of these running on screen."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Resist the urge to over-explain at this stage. The demo's power comes from showing, not telling. The students will not believe what's about to happen until they watch it. Keep this framing under three minutes.
2Paste the description — specifics, not vibes⏱ 0:03 · ~5 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Paste the full headshot generator app description into the prompt field. Scroll through it slowly so students see how detailed it is. Highlight a few specific parts as you scroll:
  • The user flow — upload, transform, preview, download
  • The Nano Banana model specified by name
  • The transformation prompt itself — paragraph-long, specifying lighting, attire, lens, depth of field
  • UI/UX requirements — color scheme, button labels, mobile responsive
🗣️ SAY "Look at how specific this is. The instinct everybody has the first time is to type 'build a photo app.' Watch what would happen with that — you'd get something that does something with photos, and you'd spend the next hour arguing with the AI about which something. The length of this description is the work. I told it the user flow, the model to use, the exact look of the output, the color scheme, the responsive behavior. Section 1.4 of the book made this point about prompts. Here it is again, doing real work."
⚠️ WATCH OUT — pacing Don't read the whole description out loud — students will tune out. Scroll, highlight, and narrate the structure of the prompt instead of its every word. The fact that it's this long is the lesson. The contents are tonight's reusable artifact, which you'll share with them after class.
3Click Build — and narrate while AI works⏱ 0:08 · ~5 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Click the Build / Generate button. The AI Studio interface will start producing project files, code, and a live preview. While it builds (usually 30–90 seconds), narrate what students are watching:
🗣️ SAY "Watch what's happening. On the left, the AI is generating a complete React application — HTML, CSS, JavaScript, the API call to Nano Banana, the responsive design, all of it. Five years ago, a developer would have charged a small business two to five thousand dollars for this and taken a couple of weeks. We're watching the same work happen in about a minute, for free, while we narrate it. This is not nothing. This is a real shift in who gets to build software — and Section 8.4 of Chapter 8 has been telling you that all week."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Do not fill the wait with silence. The longer the AI takes, the more impressive the eventual reveal will feel — but only if you're narrating the stakes. If generation stalls past two minutes, refresh once and re-trigger; don't troubleshoot live for more than 30 seconds.
4Test the preview — upload a real photo⏱ 0:13 · ~10 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN When the preview appears on the right, drag in one of your test photos. Click the Transform to Professional Headshot button. While Nano Banana processes (~15–20 seconds), continue narrating. Then show the result side by side with the original. Try a second photo — ideally one that's a harder case (poor lighting, unusual angle) to show the model's limits.
🗣️ SAY "There it is. Same person, transformed into a clean corporate headshot — studio lighting, sharp focus, neutral background, the works. Now look honestly. Did the model preserve the person's identity? Mostly, yes. Did it preserve every detail of their actual face? Almost never — and that's the Section 5.6 deepfake question made into a feature instead of a problem. That tension is what your Ethics Reflection on the Final asks you to think about: what does it mean for a person's photo to be re-rendered by a model? Whose photograph is the output, legally and morally? You will not answer those in 30 seconds, but the app gives you something concrete to argue with."
⚠️ WATCH OUT — testing honestly Try the harder photo. If it fails or distorts, do not hide that. Section 8.4's "Watch Out" is explicit: a working preview is not a finished app. A failure mid-demo is the perfect setup for the next card.
5Refine — judgment is the loop⏱ 0:23 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Go back to the prompt area and ask for one refinement live — something visible, fast, and improving. Examples:
  • "Add a loading indicator with a friendly message — 'Transforming your photo, about 20 seconds…'"
  • "Add a Reset button that clears both images."
  • "If no image is uploaded, show a hint instead of a blank box."
Watch Build Mode regenerate the relevant part. Show the updated preview.
🗣️ SAY "This refinement loop — describe, build, test, refine — is the same loop Chapter 1 sketched and Section 8.4 names as the four phases of your capstone. The first version is never the last version. The skill is not arguing with the AI; it's writing the next instruction more clearly than the last one. Tonight in GRP09 your group will run this loop on a small app of your choosing, at least once. Wednesday at 11:59 PM, you'll have done it three or four times on your individual Final — and your reflection will tell the honest story of which refinement mattered most."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Keep the refinement small and visible. Don't ask for a sweeping architectural change live — it might break the working preview and eat the rest of your demo time. Pick something students can see improve.
6Land the point — and hand off to GRP09⏱ 0:27 · ~3 min
🗣️ SAY "Three things to carry into the break. First — what you just watched is what you can do tonight, with no programming experience, for free. The barrier to creating software has changed permanently. Second — describing what you want clearly is now the work. The careful, specific prompt is where your effort goes. Third — and this is from Chapter 8 — the working preview is not the finished app. A polished-looking thing produced in 60 seconds can still hide real flaws. You verify. You click every button. You try the strange inputs. You stay in charge of the judgment. That is the whole point of the course's title."
👉 DO ON SCREEN — transition Close the headshot app. Pull up GRP09. Walk through the worksheet structure briefly — pick a problem, write a description, build, test, refine, decide. "By 9:20 every group has a working app on screen. After the break, that's your job. Breakout rooms in 15 minutes."
⚠️ WATCH OUT — if asked "can we deploy our apps live?" Be honest about the path: AI Studio Build Mode can deploy to Cloud Run, but that's a paid step requiring billing setup. For tonight's GRP09 and the Final, the live preview is the deliverable — students take a screenshot or share the AI Studio URL. Deployment is optional, and not required for full credit. Keep them focused on building, not deploying.
