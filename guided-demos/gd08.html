GD08 — Building a Gem: From Weak Instructions to Strong

Session 8 · Monday, June 8, 2026  |  Chapter 6: Language and Conversation  |  Demo tool: Gemini Gems (gemini.google.com)
Where this runs: Block A, after the Chapter 6 lecture. Paced for roughly 20 minutes. Students do not follow along — Gemini Gems is 13+ and most have a Google account, but the demo's clarity depends on you driving the screen and narrating the instructions live. Their hands-on build is in the lab.

Before class — set up the demo

This demo lands only if students feel the contrast between a weak Gem and a strong one in real time. The Résumé Reviewer is deliberately chosen because (a) every student in the room cares about résumés, and (b) it is not on the GRP07 scenario menu, so it doesn't spoil the lab.

  • Sign into gemini.google.com ahead of class. Find Gems in the left sidebar (look for "Explore Gems" or a Gems section).
  • Have a sample résumé bullet ready to paste — something short and real-looking, e.g.: 
    Responsible for managing customer service tickets and helping with team meetings as needed.
    The bullet should be classically weak: vague verbs, no outcomes, no numbers. That gives both the weak Gem and the strong Gem something to react to.
  • Have two prompts ready for the test rounds:
    1. Paste the bullet and ask: "How can I make this stronger?"
    2. Follow up with: "Should I include my high school job from 10 years ago?"
  • Mirror your screen; keep Figure 6.4 (Anatomy of a Custom Gem) open in a second tab for the framing moment.

The Demo

1Frame — a Gem is instructions wrapped around an LLM⏱ 0:00 · ~2 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Pull up Figure 6.4 from Chapter 6. Point at the three input cards: Name, Instructions, Knowledge files (optional). Point hard at Instructions and read the caption out loud: "This is the heart of it."
🗣️ SAY "An LLM does one job — predict the next plausible word. A Gem is just instructions wrapped around that LLM, shaping what counts as plausible for one specific task. Tonight I'm going to build the same Gem twice — once with weak instructions, once with strong ones — and you'll see for yourself that the AI didn't get smarter between them. I got clearer. That's the whole skill."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Resist the urge to over-explain the underlying LLM here. The chapter already did that work. The demo's job is to make instruction-writing feel concrete, not to teach the model.
2Build a weak Gem on purpose⏱ 0:02 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Open Gemini, navigate to Gems, click Create new Gem. Name it Résumé Reviewer. In the Instructions field, type a deliberately thin instruction: 
Help me with my résumé.
Save. Open a chat with the Gem. Paste the weak bullet and ask: "How can I make this stronger?"
🗣️ SAY "Watch what happens. The instruction was four words. Gemini will give us something — it always will, because that's what LLMs do — but watch the shape of the answer."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Read the response out loud at full pace. Do not paraphrase it down. The point is that the response is fluent, polite, vague, and gives six bullet points instead of one decision. The students need to feel the wishy-washiness, not just be told about it.
3Push the weak Gem until it cracks⏱ 0:06 · ~3 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Ask the second prompt in the same chat: "Should I include my high school job from 10 years ago?" Read the response out loud.
🗣️ SAY "Six bullet points. On the one hand, on the other hand. A perfectly fluent way of saying nothing. This is not a bad model — this is Gemini, the same Gemini you've all used. It hedges because I never told it who it is. There's no recruiter in this room. There's no opinion in this room. There's just me with four words of instruction asking an LLM to be specific, and an LLM that has no idea what specific looks like for this job."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Do not argue with the Gem. Do not try to coax a better answer out of it by prompting harder. That is exactly the wrong instinct, and showing students that instinct fail is the demo's whole pedagogical point. Close the chat.
4Rewrite with a real persona and constraints⏱ 0:09 · ~7 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Go back to the Gem's settings and replace the instructions with this — type it live, narrating each section. The narration is the demo: 
ROLE
You are a senior technical recruiter at a Fortune 500 company who has
reviewed 10,000+ résumés for entry- and mid-level technical roles.

PERSONA
Direct, candid, generous with concrete examples. You don't hedge. You decide.

INPUTS YOU EXPECT
The user will paste a résumé bullet, section, or whole résumé and ask
for feedback.

OUTPUTS YOU PRODUCE
For each piece of input, you respond in three labeled parts:
  1. THE REWRITE — your improved version of the weakest line.
  2. THE ONE THING TO ADD — the single missing element that would move
     them up the recruiter's pile.
  3. THE SKIM CHECK — any line a recruiter would skim past, and why.

MUST ALWAYS
- Push for outcomes over duties. "Cut ticket response time 40%" beats
  "responsible for tickets."
- Name concrete numbers, tools, or scope wherever the user could plausibly add them.
- Stay brief — recruiters skim, so do you.

MUST NEVER
- Open with "Great résumé!" or any other recruiter-doesn't-talk-this-way pleasantry.
- Give five-option lists when one answer is the right answer.
- Invent achievements the user did not mention.
Save. Open a fresh chat with the Gem. Paste the SAME bullet from Step 2 and ask the SAME first question: "How can I make this stronger?"
🗣️ SAY "Notice what I did. I gave it a role — a specific kind of person with a specific kind of authority. I gave it a persona — direct, no hedging. I told it exactly what to expect in, exactly what to produce out, and the things it must and must never do. None of this is rocket science. All of it is just me getting clearer. Now watch the same model, same conversation, give us something completely different."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Do not skip the narration. The visible structure of the instructions — six labeled sections — is what students will replicate in the lab. If you type it as one paragraph, they'll replicate that.
5Land the point — and hand off to the lab⏱ 0:16 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Run the SECOND question from Step 3: "Should I include my high school job from 10 years ago?" Let the strong Gem answer. It should now decide — usually "no, unless X."
🗣️ SAY "Look at what just happened. Same model. Same conversation pattern. Different Gem, because different instructions. The Gem that hedged is gone — this one decides. And the only thing that changed is what I wrote. Three takeaways for tonight's lab. First — the chapter is right: the instructions are the heart of it. Second — when a Gem isn't behaving, you do not argue with it; you go back and edit the instructions. Third — a Gem worth building is one a real person, in a real job, would actually use. That's what GRP07 asks you to do — pick a professional role, name a recurring task, and write the instructions that make the Gem useful in that specific context."
👉 DO ON SCREEN — transition Pull up GRP07. Walk them through the six-part scaffold on screen — they will see the same labels (ROLE, PERSONA, INPUTS, OUTPUTS, MUST ALWAYS, MUST NEVER) you just used. Send them to breakouts.
⚠️ WATCH OUT — if asked "do we have to use those six labels?" Be direct: yes, tonight. The scaffold is the lesson. Once they have done it deliberately once, they can structure their own way. Tonight is the structured rep.
