GD02 — NotebookLM: Research That Cites Its Sources

Session 3 · Monday, May 18, 2026  |  Chapter 2: Digital Literacy and Data  |  Featured tool: NotebookLM
Where this runs: Block A, after the welcome and lecture. The walkthrough below is paced for roughly 45 minutes. It mirrors Chapter 2, Section 2.6 — students have read it, so the demo is you showing them what they read.

Before class — set up the demo notebook

NotebookLM answers only from sources you add, so the demo is only as good as the notebook you walk in with. Build it in advance:

  • Sign in to notebooklm.google.com with your MDC Google account ahead of time.
  • Create one notebook and curate about four trustworthy sources on a single question. Suggested topic, because it reinforces Section 2.3: "Has the Internet of Things actually made cities better?" — e.g., a city government smart-city page, a reputable tech or engineering explainer, a research overview, and one short explainer video. Swap in your own topic if you prefer; keep it to one focused question.
  • Have one citation already clicked once so you know which passage it jumps to.
  • Optional but recommended: start an Audio Overview generating right before class so it is ready when you reach Card 4 — it takes a minute or two.
  • Mirror your screen and keep the Chapter 2 page open in a second tab.

The Walkthrough

1Open NotebookLM and create a notebook⏱ 0:00 · ~3 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Go to notebooklm.google.com, signed in. Click Create new (or New notebook). Give it a clear name tied to the question.
🗣️ SAY "Last chapter we met Gemini — it answers from everything it was trained on, and that is why it can drift into a confident guess. NotebookLM is the opposite tool. It answers only from sources we hand it, and it shows us exactly where each answer came from. Same kind of model underneath — pointed at a different job."
⚠️ WATCH OUT A student may not be able to follow along yet — that is fine, this card is just you. Reassure them: as of May 2026 NotebookLM is free with any Google account and open to anyone 13 or older, so everyone can use it in the lab.
2Add your curated sources⏱ 0:03 · ~6 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Open the Sources panel and click Add. Show the range of source types as you add your four: upload a PDF, paste a website address, link a Google Doc, and add a video. Let students watch each one get ingested.
🗣️ SAY "This is the most important step in the whole tool. The five questions from Section 2.2 — who made it, how current, what evidence, what purpose, does it hold up — that is the work that happens before a source ever lands here. Curate before you add. Feed it four weak sources and it will give you beautifully cited weak answers."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Flag the ethics point now, not later: never upload someone else's private material — patient records, a colleague's file, confidential business documents — into a personal AI tool.
3Ask questions — and click the citations⏱ 0:09 · ~8 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN In the Chat panel, ask two or three plain-language questions, e.g. "What are the main benefits this material identifies?" and "Summarize the concerns raised across all the sources." When an answer appears, click a numbered citation — it jumps to the exact passage in the source. Click two or three.
🗣️ SAY "Every claim comes with a numbered citation. Clicking one takes you to the sentence that backs it. That click is you doing Section 2.2's question 3 — what is the evidence? — in about one second. Get in the habit of clicking them."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Resist answering from your own knowledge. If a question's answer is not in the sources, that is the demo working — save it; you will use exactly that behavior in Card 6.
4Tour the Studio panel⏱ 0:17 · ~12 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Open the Studio panel and show what it can build from the same sources:
  • Audio Overview — a podcast-style discussion of the sources (the one you pre-generated; play 15–20 seconds).
  • Video Overview — a narrated visual summary.
  • Mind Map — click into a node to expand it.
  • Slides — a generated deck of the key points.
  • Reports / Briefing doc — a written summary of the sources.
  • Flashcards and Quiz — study and self-check material.
🗣️ SAY "Every one of these is generated from your curated sources — so unlike a generic AI, it stays on your topic. The Audio Overview is a study podcast for the commute. The flashcards and quiz are made from your actual material, not a textbook average."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Generation takes a minute or two — never wait on it live. Pre-start the Audio Overview, and while a second item generates, keep talking or click into the mind map.
5Prompt the Studio apps & the outputs → notes → sources loop⏱ 0:29 · ~7 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Show that Studio outputs are steerable: when generating an Audio Overview, report, or quiz, open Customize and give a short focus instruction — "focus on the safety section," "explain it for a beginner," "make the quiz harder." Then show the loop: save a strong chat answer or Studio output as a Note → select your notes → convert a note into a source.
🗣️ SAY "Here is the loop that makes this powerful. You ask a question, you get a cited answer, you save the good ones as Notes. Then your notes — your own synthesis — can become sources themselves. The notebook gets richer over time, and it stays grounded the whole way, because everything traces back to material you chose."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Controls and labels shift between NotebookLM updates. Tell students plainly: if a button has moved, find the equivalent — the idea (steer it, save it, reuse it) does not change.
6Show what NotebookLM will NOT do⏱ 0:36 · ~4 min
👉 DO ON SCREEN Ask the notebook a question your sources clearly do not cover. Let the class see it say the answer is not in the material rather than invent one.
🗣️ SAY "Watch this. It is refusing — and that refusal is the tool working correctly. Ask Gemini the same thing and it will happily produce a confident answer with no sources behind it. That difference is the whole point of grounding."
⚠️ WATCH OUT Do not let "it cites its sources" become "so it must be true." Say it directly: a citation shows you where a claim came from, not that the claim is correct. Garbage sources produce well-cited garbage. The judgment stays with the person who chose the sources.
7NotebookLM + Gemini, then open the use-case discussion⏱ 0:40 · ~5 min
🗣️ SAY "The two tools are a pair. The workflow you'll use all term: research grounded in NotebookLM — gather credible sources, ask until you understand, every claim cited — then draft with Gemini, which is stronger at shaping writing. NotebookLM keeps the facts honest; Gemini helps with the words."
👉 DO ON SCREEN — run the use-case discussion Put up these uses and ask the room which fits their own field, then ask for ideas of their own:
  • Healthcare student — load assigned readings and lecture notes; get a hard concept explained and a practice quiz, all cited to the actual course material.
  • Small-business owner — drop in three vendor contracts; ask for a plain comparison of the cancellation terms, each point traceable to the contract.
  • Skilled trades — load an equipment manual and a safety standard; ask for the exact procedure for a task, cited to the page.
  • Anyone facing a dense document — a lease, a benefits packet, a policy: add it and ask plain questions instead of guessing.
⚠️ WATCH OUT — transition Keep the discussion to a few minutes, then hand off to Block B: pull up the Group Lab (GRP02) link and send students to breakout rooms to build a grounded notebook of their own.
