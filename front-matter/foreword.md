I have taught this course many times, and it always begins the same way: a room of people who are not sure they belong there.

Some are sixteen years old, taking a college class for the first time. Some are returning to school after twenty years away from a classroom. A few already write code for a living; far more have never written a line, and quietly suspect that "artificial intelligence" is a subject for other people — younger people, or more technical people, or people who were let in on a secret they somehow missed.

This book is written to tell every one of those students the same thing: **you belong here, and this subject is yours.**

Artificial intelligence is the most talked-about technology of our moment, and most of the talk is unhelpful. One headline promises it will end work as we know it; the next dismisses it as a parlor trick. Both cannot be right, and the noise leaves intelligent, capable people feeling they must either worship the technology or fear it. There is a third option, and it is the whole purpose of this book: **understand it.** Understand it well enough to use it for real work, to judge the claims made about it, and to keep your own judgment firmly in charge.

That is what the title means. *Mind the machine* — pay close attention to it, learn what it is actually doing, and never hand it the wheel of your own thinking.

The book runs on a simple loop, and every chapter repeats it. First we **demystify** — we take a piece of hype and cut it down to what is real. Then we look at **how the technology actually works**, explained in plain language, with no assumed background. Then you **apply it** — you use a current, free AI tool on a problem from your own life, studies, or work. And finally you **analyze** — you step back and ask what the tool got right, what it got wrong, and what your own part in the result was. Demystify, understand, apply, analyze: eight chapters, eight weeks, one habit of mind.

You will not be asked to take anything on faith — not a headline's word, not a company's, and not mine. You will be asked to try things, to break them, and to think honestly about what happened. The tools in this book are free, and chosen so that every student can use them whatever their age or budget. The examples are drawn from nursing and business, the trades and the classroom, and ordinary daily life — because artificial intelligence has reached every one of those worlds, and not one of them belongs to someone else.

A last word — the same one I give every class on day one. This material is not as hard as the hype makes it sound, and not as harmless as the cynics claim. It is a powerful, ordinary tool, and like any powerful tool it rewards the person who takes the time to understand it. Take that time. Be curious, be a little skeptical, and be honest about what confuses you. That is not weakness; it is exactly how this is done.

Welcome to AI Thinking. Let's begin.

— Professor Carlos Marquez
