This book was designed to be *used*, not just read. A few minutes here will help you get the most out of it.

## The shape of a chapter

There are eight chapters, one for each week of the course, and every chapter follows the same four-beat rhythm:

1. **Demystify.** The chapter opens on a piece of AI hype — a headline or a myth — and takes it apart.
2. **Understand.** It explains how the technology actually works, in plain language, with every term defined the first time it appears.
3. **Apply.** You use a real, current AI tool on a problem of your own.
4. **Analyze.** You step back and judge what happened — what the tool did well, where it failed, and what that means.

Each chapter opens with a full-page infographic that previews everything in it, a short list of **learning objectives**, and numbered sections (8.1, 8.2, and so on). It closes with a summary, the key terms, and a bridge to what comes next. Read the assigned chapter *before* class — the class session builds on it.

## The callouts

Throughout each chapter you will find boxed notes. Each kind has a job:

- 🔍 **Myth vs. Reality** — a common belief about AI, and what is actually true.
- ✋ **Try This** — a small hands-on task you can do in a few minutes, often right where you are reading.
- 🛠️ **In Practice** — a worked, real-world example of the idea.
- ⚠️ **Watch Out** — a common mistake or risk, and how to avoid it.
- ⚖️ **Ethics Check** — the ethical questions the chapter's topic raises.
- 🔎 **Going Deeper** — an optional, more technical dropdown. Open it if you want more; skip it with no loss if you do not. The book works at two depths, and you choose yours.

## The end-of-chapter work

Every chapter closes with a section called **Apply and Analyze**, which contains:

- **Review Questions** — five quick questions with an answer key, to check your understanding.
- **A Discussion Question** — one deeper, open-ended question.
- **A Breakout Lab** — a short group activity, done in class, where you apply the chapter's tool to a real problem. It is low-stakes; the point is to *try*.
- **An Optional Take-Home** — a longer reflection, assigned at your instructor's discretion, that adds ethics and bias to what you built.

Chapters 4 and 6 also include an **Optional Advanced Lab** with an "Open in Colab" button — a free, browser-based Python notebook for anyone who wants to see the code behind the ideas. It is genuinely optional: no programming experience is expected anywhere in this book.

## The tools, and a note on age

You will use real AI tools, not toy versions. Every tool you are *required* to use is **free** — and free in a way that works for every member of the class, either with no account at all or with a free account open to anyone 13 and older.

A few advanced tools carry an 18-and-over age requirement. Where one of those appears, your instructor demonstrates it, and you use a free, age-open tool for your own work. **No student is ever shut out of an activity.** Because AI tools change quickly, every tool walkthrough carries a "Verified as of" date, and **Addendum E** keeps a living quick-reference — name, cost, age policy — for all of them.

## Read it like a skeptic

One last thing. This book asks you to be a little skeptical — of AI, of the headlines, and of the book itself. When a chapter makes a claim, test it. When a tool hands you an answer, check it. That habit is not a side lesson. It is the whole subject.
