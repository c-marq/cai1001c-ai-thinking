SECTION 1Plan Your App part of app score

✅ DO THIS Complete this section before you open AI Studio. The chapter is right: the work is in defining the problem clearly. A 10-minute planning sprint here saves you an hour of refinement later.

1. The real problem

One or two sentences. Not "improve productivity" — "my mom runs a small Cuban café and spends 20 min every night calculating the next day's bread order based on yesterday's sales. She does this on paper."

2. The user — name them

Who specifically would use this? "My mom (Marta, 58, runs Café Versailles in Hialeah)." Vague users produce vague apps.

3. What the user enters

List every input. Be specific about format. "Yesterday's bread sales (a number), today's day of week (a dropdown), and any special event for tomorrow (a free-text note)."

4. What the app shows / does

The output, exactly. "A suggested bread order for tomorrow with a sentence of reasoning, broken out by bread type."

5. Look and feel

Color scheme, mobile responsiveness, button style. "Warm colors — terracotta and cream. Large readable text. Mobile-first since she'll use her phone."

6. Edge cases — what should happen when things are weird

"If yesterday's sales were zero, suggest a starter quantity. If she enters an event, factor it into the suggestion. Never suggest fewer than 5 loaves."

SECTION 3Documentation 30 pts

✅ DO THIS Write 150–200 words documenting your app for someone who has never seen it before. This is the README a colleague or your future self would want to read. Cover all four points below in clear paragraphs.
💡 COVER ALL FOUR POINTS
  1. Purpose and user. What problem does the app solve, and for whom?
  2. How it works. Plain-language explanation of what the app does internally, including which AI model or capability does the work (Nano Banana, Gemini text, etc.).
  3. How to use it. Step-by-step user flow — what someone clicks, types, and sees, in order.
  4. Known limitations. Specific things your app does not handle well, and why. Honesty here is graded; "no limitations" is a red flag.

SECTION 4Ethics & Bias Reflection 30 pts

✅ DO THIS Write 200+ words on the ethics of your app — not a generic ethics essay. Engage at least three of the six ethics principles from Chapter 8 Section 8.3, and if your app uses generated content, include at least one explicit reference to Chapter 7 (provenance, watermarking, the "study the seams" idea). Argue, don't list. Pick a position and defend it.
💡 THE SIX ETHICS PRINCIPLES (CH 8 SECTION 8.3) Pick at least three to apply specifically to your app. Don't just define them — name the decision you made (or didn't make) about each.
  1. Bias — what was your AI model trained on, and how could that bias show up in your app's output?
  2. Privacy — does your app handle personal data? Whose? Would they consent?
  3. Fairness — does your app work as well for every group it's meant to serve? How would you measure?
  4. Accountability — if your app gives a wrong or harmful output, who's responsible? You? The user? Google? Name it.
  5. Transparency — does the user know AI is involved? Do they know how it makes decisions?
  6. Human oversight — for the consequential decisions your app supports, can a human review and overrule it? Should they?
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Writing a generic "ethics is important" essay that could apply to any app. The rubric rewards specificity: this app, these three principles, this decision I made or am still wrestling with. Cite a section number from Ch 7 or Ch 8 when you reference an idea. Make it clear you have read the chapters.

Troubleshooting & FAQs

QuestionAnswer
Can I use Gemini Canvas instead of AI Studio Build Mode?Yes — same paradigm, simpler interface, prompts via gemini.google.com. Pick one, stick with it. Section 8.4 of the chapter walks through Canvas if you want a reference.
Does my app have to be deployed to a public URL?No. The live preview in AI Studio (or the Canvas link) counts as the deliverable. Deployment to Cloud Run is optional and not graded.
What if my app idea is "too small"?It probably isn't. Section 8.4 makes the case: "the best capstones are not ambitious — they are specific." A working tip splitter beats a half-built tutor every time.
Can I use real personal data (medical, financial) to test?No. Section 8.4 is explicit: "never put genuinely private data into an app you are still testing." Use test data.
The app worked in my GRP09 build but Build Mode is producing different code today.Build Mode is non-deterministic. Save a working version's link or code immediately when you have something good. Don't rely on regenerating the same result.
What if Build Mode is down on the day I'm working?Switch to Gemini Canvas as your fallback. The five-part description scaffold works in either tool.
How strict is the 5-minute presentation cap?Strict. With 10–15 presenters, every minute over yours is a minute someone else loses. Rehearse with a timer.
What if I can't present live on Wed 6/17?Email your instructor by Mon 6/15 with documentation of your conflict. If approved, you submit a 5-minute recorded video instead. Recorded fallback is not an opt-out — it's an accommodation.
